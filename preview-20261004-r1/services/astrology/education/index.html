Education, study and exams

For exam trouble, a student who cannot focus, a choice of stream, or a child's studies.

What a study reading cannot say

A chart is not an assessment of ability, and a reading here is not a learning evaluation. It cannot say how intelligent a student is, whether they have a learning difference, or how they will score. It makes no promise of marks or of admission.

One test bears directly on this. In a test run in Pune in 2008 and reported in Current Science in 2009, practitioners were asked to tell the charts of bright children from the charts of children with learning disabilities. As a group they did no better than chance. It was a single test, organised with a rationalist group, so it is not the last word, but it is a fair reason not to rely on a chart as a measure of ability.

If a student is sleeping badly, not eating, or talking about harming themselves, please see a doctor straight away, or call Tele-MANAS on 14416, the national mental health helpline, which answers at any hour. None of this is medical or mental-health advice.

Marksheets, deadlines and the student’s own words

  • The student’s birth date and place, with the time as closely as anyone knows it. The D24 depends on the minute.
  • Recent marksheets or teachers’ comments, and the subjects that feel hardest and easiest.
  • Anything with a deadline: an exam, a form to be filed, a counselling round.
  • In the student’s own words, what they would like from the conversation.

What students and parents bring

Most arrive with one of a few worries. A student is anxious before exams, or does well at home and loses it in the hall, or is disappointed by a result. Another cannot focus, though they follow the lesson. A third faces a choice that feels too big: a stream after Class 10, a degree after Class 12, a first profession.

Parents bring their own: why a bright child is struggling, how to support one who has not found an interest yet, whether a young person should go abroad to study. Some bring a competitive entrance test that has taken years of preparation, and some bring a gap, such as a year out or an attempt that did not go as hoped.

These are associations, not measures. A strong Mercury in a chart does not make a student quick, and a quiet one does not make them slow. The list is useful as a way to ask what the student enjoys and where effort goes to waste.

For education, Jyotish also uses a finer chart, the D24, which splits each sign into twenty-four parts of one degree and fifteen minutes. Its rising sign changes every few minutes, so it needs a birth time you can trust. Where the time is uncertain, a reading leans on the main chart and says so.

A child’s chart, and the parents’ part

When a parent asks about a child, the chart is one voice in the room, and not the one with most to say. The child has a say, and so do their teachers, their marksheets and their own interests. A reading that leaves out the child’s view is a reading of the parent’s worry.

Tradition reads a child’s chart through the same houses and planets. For a young student that gives a way of noticing what draws them, how they take to effort and where they dig in. It describes leanings. It does not forecast results.

A parent’s part

  • Ask before advising, and listen to the answer.
  • Keep comparisons with cousins and classmates out of it.
  • Notice the effort and the method as well as the marks.
  • Do not hand a child a label from a chart. A line such as ‘weak at maths’ can stick.
  • Let the child be present when their path is discussed.

None of this replaces the people who see the child every day. If a child seems unhappy, avoids school, or is not eating or sleeping well, a doctor or a school counsellor should be the first call.

Focus and study habits, with the mind first

When a student cannot focus, Narayani starts with the minutes before attention goes, ahead of the chart. She asks what the student tells themselves (‘I will not understand this’), what they do next (pick up the phone, tidy the desk) and what that buys them, which is usually a few minutes of relief. Losing focus can be a way of stepping out of a feeling of not being good enough, and that feeling is worth noticing.

Exam nerves follow a similar loop. The student expects to forget, the expectation brings tension, and a blank, when it comes, seems to prove the expectation right. Narayani looks at where in that loop a student can do something different, such as arriving with a plan for the first five minutes of the paper.

Ordinary causes come first. Poor sleep, a phone within reach, hunger, worry at home and a subject taught in a way that does not reach the student all take focus away, and none of them is in a chart. Trouble that lasts, or that runs across reading, writing, numbers or attention in every subject, deserves an assessment from a doctor, a paediatrician or a qualified psychologist. A reading is not one and should not stand in for it.

A caution about positive sentences: when people with low self-esteem repeated one in experiments, they came away feeling worse. A statement a student can accept, such as ‘I did two chapters today’, is easier to believe than a boast like ‘I am brilliant’. The Brain Training pages cover practices of this kind and what research shows about each.

Timing exams and admissions, in a general sense

A board sets the date of an exam and a counselling round sets the date of an admission, and no chart moves either. What Jyotish offers is a view of the stretch around them: the main period and sub-period running through the student’s year, and where Saturn and Jupiter stand when counted from the birth Moon.

Tradition links Mercury and Jupiter with learning and Saturn with discipline and sustained effort, so a Saturn stretch is read as a time when steady work counts for more than bursts of it. A reading might note this and ask what it means in practice: a steadier timetable, fewer commitments, more rest before a paper.

Choosing a good day for a first class is an old custom, and it stays a custom. No date for a result, a rank or a seat can be read from a chart, and a student should never be told that one can.

Choosing a stream, course or profession

In Jyotish the ninth house stands for higher study, the tenth for profession and the D10 for public role, with Mercury and Jupiter for the mind that does the work. These are prompts, not verdicts, and a stream decision rests on more than any of them.

A sensible order for a stream decision

  1. 1

    Start with the student

    What they do without being asked, and what they lose track of time over.

  2. 2

    Look at the evidence

    Marksheets, teachers’ comments, and an aptitude assessment from a qualified counsellor if the choice is a large one.

  3. 3

    Read the chart as a prompt

    Use the houses, planets and D10 to ask questions, never to choose.

  4. 4

    Test before committing

    A week of real work in the subject, a conversation with someone in the profession, or a sample paper.

  5. 5

    Decide together

    The student’s view carries the most weight, and the decision stays with the family.

The chart comes third because a sentence such as ‘science suits you’, once spoken, colours everything after it, and a student may stop looking. For work after study, the career and business page goes further into the tenth house and the D10.

Study abroad, competitive exams and gaps

Study abroad
Tradition reads the ninth house for higher study and the twelfth for residence abroad. A reading can help a family think about readiness and about what the move asks of everyone. Fees, visas and admissions belong to the institutions and advisers involved.
Competitive entrance tests
Years of preparation for one sitting, where the pressure is often heavier than the syllabus. The conversation is about how the preparation is being carried. The student’s own practice papers say more than any chart does.
A gap or a pause
A year out after a result, a second try that fell short, a degree set down unfinished. A gap is a fact in a path and says nothing about the student’s worth. A reading can look at what the student learned in it, and what picking the path up again would need.

Questions people ask

If yours is not here, ask it when you enquire.

Ask something else
What does an education consultation cover?

It begins with the student’s account, or the parent’s if the student is young, and the chart comes after that. Narayani also asks about daily habits of study and the thoughts around them. The aim is a sharper picture of leanings, timing and next steps. It is not an assessment of ability, and it promises no marks.

Does a stone or bead come into a study reading?

Tradition pairs Mercury, the planet of the intellect, with emerald and Jupiter, the planet of knowledge, with yellow sapphire, which is why these two come up in questions about study. No study has shown a stone improving focus or exam results, and Narayani does not offer one as a route to marks. Marks come from preparation, rest and the paper on the day. The Gemstones page explains what to check before any purchase.

Can astrology help with exam trouble or choosing a stream or profession?

It can open a conversation, though it cannot deliver a result. For exam trouble, the reading asks how the student meets pressure and looks at the stretch the exam falls in. For a stream, it offers leanings to check against marksheets, interests and an honest trial of the subject. It cannot raise marks, choose a stream for anyone or promise admission, and the student’s own view carries the most weight.

Why can’t I focus on my studies, and can a chart say anything about it?

A chart cannot diagnose a focus problem. In Jyotish the Moon has charge of the mind and Mercury of the intellect, so they are a place to start talking and no more. The common causes are everyday ones, such as poor sleep, a phone within reach or worry, so Narayani asks first what happens just before attention drifts. If focus is hard in every subject, or has been for a long time, please ask a doctor or a qualified psychologist.

More questions (3)
Can a chart show whether my child will pass a competitive exam or get into a particular college?

No. A rank depends on the paper, the competition and the day, and none of these is in a chart. A reading can look at how a student is carrying a long preparation, and which stretches of the year may ask for more rest or more effort. It makes no promise about marks, a rank or admission.

My child’s chart and my child’s wishes point in different directions. Which should we follow?

Start with the wishes, and test them against evidence: sustained interest, marksheets, and an honest trial of the subject. A chart is a prompt for conversation, not evidence about ability. Narayani uses it to open questions, and the decision stays with the family and, above all, with the student, who is the one who will do the studying.

I did not pass an exam, or I took a gap year. Does my chart show a problem?

No. A setback is an event in a path, not a final judgment. A reading can look at the stretch you went through, ask what the time taught you, and think about how to take the path up again. It does not read failure into a chart, and it does not promise success in the next attempt.
