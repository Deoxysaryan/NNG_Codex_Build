A stone or a bead often reaches you as a name, a weight and a price from someone else, with a promise attached. Many people want to know whether any of it is sound before they spend.

Start with your question, not a purchase. The guides explain traditional associations, authenticity checks and the limits of the evidence, so you can weigh the advice you receive.

What she weighs before any stone or bead comes up

  • What is prompting the question: a warning someone gave you, a worry, or plain curiosity
  • Your chart: the rising sign, the planet in question and the period now running
  • Your numbers, where they bear on the question
  • Any stone or bead you already wear, who advised it, and how it has gone
  • What a purchase would mean for your budget and your household

What a ratna and a Rudraksha bead are

A ratna is a gem. Jyotish, the Indian tradition of astrology, links nine of them, the navaratna, with the nine grahas, or planets. The Gemstones page gives the nine and says where the pairing comes from.

Rudraksha beads are seeds from Elaeocarpus trees. They are strung into malas for prayer and counting, and the name ties them to Rudra, a name of Shiva. Each bead has natural grooves, called mukhi, or faces.

The word crystal causes confusion. In shops it often means the ornamental stones of modern crystal practice, such as quartz or tiger’s eye, chosen by intention, sign or birth month. Gemstone, on these pages, means the nine ratna, which tradition reads from the chart.

Why a stone is never where she starts

She starts with you and your question, and with what is going on in your mind, before any object comes up. She does not tell anyone that something will go wrong if a stone is not worn.

A conversation cannot test a stone or a bead. For a stone, the usual proof of what it is comes from an independent laboratory. For a bead, the check sellers rate highest is an X-ray or CT image of its inner chambers.

What the evidence shows about benefit claims

Most of what is said about stones and beads is belief. The table sets the common claims beside what has been tested.

If a seller ties a stone or bead to an illness, a debt or a court case, take that as the cue to stop and ask for evidence. A doctor, a lawyer or a qualified financial adviser is the right person for those questions, and no stone replaces them.

Lists and labels you will meet, and what they rest on

Shops, videos and friends offer many ways to choose a stone. Most are modern overlays, and it helps to know which is which.

Explore lists and labels
  • Birth-month stones are a Western birthstone custom. The modern American list was set in 1912 and has been revised since, and it is not a Jyotish method.
  • Lists by Sun sign leave most of the chart out, because Jyotish ties stones to planets and reads the whole chart. Lists by ruling number come from numerology guides and shops, and they are a separate scheme from the chart-based method.
  • Chakra and element pairings are modern overlays on the nine-stone tradition. Chakras come from yoga and tantra, they are not a medical concept, and no matching structure in the body has been established.
  • Carrying a stone, holding it in meditation, setting an intention, charging it in moonlight and choosing one for a goal such as calm or love come from modern crystal practice. No effect has been measured, and some stones fade in strong light.
  • A stone given as a gift is a keepsake. A stone chosen from a chart was chosen for one person.
  • Some minerals are toxic, so a stone should not be soaked in water you mean to drink.

Prices, supply and what to ask before you pay

The same stone can cost very different amounts from different sellers, because size, quality, treatment and rarity all move the price, and none of them measures an effect. Whoever you buy from, settle the terms in writing first.

Explore price and supply

These are information guides, not a shop. Ask the team what guidance is available and what it includes before booking or buying anything.

Before you pay for anything, anywhere

  • What exactly it is: natural, treated or laboratory-grown, stated in writing.
  • Who says so: an independent laboratory, or only the seller, and whether the laboratory’s report costs extra.
  • The return terms, in writing, and how long they last.
  • Whether any claim is made for health, money or luck. If so, take it as a reason to ask for the evidence.

Questions people ask

If yours is not here, ask it when you enquire.

Ask something else
Do I need a gemstone or a Rudraksha bead at all?

No. Tradition offers many remedies, and a stone or bead is only one of them. A good number of people never wear either, and many conversations end with a decision to make, a talk to have or a habit to build, and nothing to put on.

How is a stone chosen for a particular person?

Tradition reads the whole birth chart, starting from the planet that rules the rising sign and the period now running, and does not choose from a Sun sign or a birth month. There is no beginner stone. Practitioners weigh the factors differently, and no test of any method was found, so ask for the reasoning as well as the name. The Gemstones page works through an example.

What does a laboratory certificate tell me?

It names the stone, says whether it is natural or laboratory-grown, lists the treatments the laboratory could detect, and gives the weight and size. It carries no price, it cannot show that an undetectable treatment is absent, and it says nothing about your chart. A report written by the seller proves only what the seller says. The Gemstones page covers what a report shows and leaves out.

What is the difference between natural, treated and lab-grown?

Natural means formed in the earth and then cut and polished. Treated means a natural stone changed after it was found, by heat, oil, dye or filling. Lab-grown means grown in a laboratory with the same chemistry as the natural stone, so the eye cannot separate them. Imitation means another material altogether, such as glass. Ask for the kind in writing. The Gemstones page explains each.

How can I tell that a Rudraksha is genuine?

Look at the grooves first. On a real bead they run unbroken from end to end, and carved extras are a known copy. Sellers rate an X-ray or CT image highest, since the inner chambers should match the grooves, and they add that even an image can mislead without an expert reading. The water test is not conclusive. The Rudraksha page sets out the checks and the copies in full.

Can anyone wear Rudraksha?

The Shiva Purana sets no limit of class, stage of life or sex. Conditions about diet and menstruation come from modern guides, who disagree with one another, so regard them as household custom. A loose small bead can choke a young child. The Rudraksha page covers who may wear it, and what is and is not claimed for it.

Is there a trial period, and should I take one?

Some guides suggest wearing a stone for a few days before you commit, with the length ranging from three days to two weeks or more. No evidence shows that a trial reveals whether a stone suits you, but it can serve as a cooling-off period if the return terms are agreed in writing before you pay. The Gemstones page sets out how to run one.

Will a stone work straight away?

No timeline can be given. Guides quote anything from a day or two to many weeks, and no study found shows a stone itself producing an effect, so a date would be a promise nobody can keep. If you feel steadier after putting one on, the ritual and the attention it brings may be the reason, which is worth noticing.

Can a stone or bead help with an illness, stress or money worries?

No study found shows that it can. In the controlled trials on crystals, belief explained the effect, and no trial of people wearing a bead was found. Symptoms belong with a doctor, and any treatment you already have should continue. If money is the worry, a qualified financial adviser is the person to ask, and nothing on this page is advice of either kind.

Will I be pushed to buy a stone or bead?

No. Her advice does not depend on any purchase, and a conversation can end with nothing to buy and nothing to wear. If you do decide to buy, from anyone, settle in writing what the stone is, who says so and what the return terms are, and take whatever time you need.
