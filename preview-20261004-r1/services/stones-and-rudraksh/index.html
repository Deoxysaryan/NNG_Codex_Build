Read the background: the introduction

A stone or a bead often reaches you as a name, a weight and a price from someone else, with a promise attached. Many people want to know whether any of it is sound before they spend.

Start with your question, not a purchase. The guides explain traditional associations, authenticity checks and the limits of the evidence, so you can weigh the advice you receive.

What she weighs before any stone or bead comes up

  • What is prompting the question: a warning someone gave you, a worry, or plain curiosity
  • Your chart: the rising sign, the planet in question and the period now running
  • Your numbers, where they bear on the question
  • Any stone or bead you already wear, who advised it, and how it has gone
  • What a purchase would mean for your budget and your household

What a ratna and a Rudraksha bead are

Gemstones and Rudraksha beads have different origins and uses. Rudraksha beads have natural grooves. Their traditional associations are not proof of a benefit.

Why a stone is never where she starts

She starts with you and your question, and with what is going on in your mind, before any object comes up. She does not tell anyone that something will go wrong if a stone is not worn.

What she helps with, and what no conversation can test

What the evidence shows about benefit claims

No reliable evidence establishes that wearing a stone or bead changes health, money or relationships. Authenticity checks tell you what you are buying, not what it will do.

If a seller ties a stone or bead to an illness, a debt or a court case, take that as the cue to stop and ask for evidence. A doctor, a lawyer or a qualified financial adviser is the right person for those questions, and no stone replaces them.

Lists and labels you will meet, and what they rest on

Labels such as birthstone, chakra stone or energised describe different traditions or selling conventions. Ask what a label actually means before paying more for it.

Prices, supply and what to ask before you pay

The same stone can cost very different amounts from different sellers, because size, quality, treatment and rarity all move the price, and none of them measures an effect. Whoever you buy from, settle the terms in writing first.

These are information guides, not a shop. Ask the team what guidance is available and what it includes before booking or buying anything.

Before you pay for anything, anywhere

  • What exactly it is: natural, treated or laboratory-grown, stated in writing.
  • Who says so: an independent laboratory, or only the seller, and whether the laboratory’s report costs extra.
  • The return terms, in writing, and how long they last.
  • Whether any claim is made for health, money or luck. If so, take it as a reason to ask for the evidence.

Before you enquire: what to gather

  • Your birth date, time and place, because the chart is where any stone advice starts
  • A clear photograph of any stone or bead you own, with any receipt or paper that came with it
  • Any warning or deadline that came with the advice, in the words it was put to you

Is this for you?

It may suit you if

  • someone has told you to wear a stone or bead, and you want the reasoning before you decide
  • you already own one and want to know what to ask about it
  • you would rather hear that nothing is needed, if that turns out to be the answer

It may not be right if

  • you want a promise that a stone will bring luck, money or relief
  • you are hoping a stone will help with symptoms or a diagnosis, which belong with a doctor
  • you want a stone named for you without a conversation about your question

Ask something else
Do I need a gemstone or a Rudraksha bead at all?

No. Tradition offers many remedies, and a stone or bead is only one of them. A good number of people never wear either, and many conversations end with a decision to make, a talk to have or a habit to build, and nothing to put on.

How is a stone chosen for a particular person?

Tradition reads the whole birth chart, starting from the planet that rules the rising sign and the period now running, and does not choose from a Sun sign or a birth month. There is no beginner stone. Practitioners weigh the factors differently, and no test of any method was found, so ask for the reasoning as well as the name. The Gemstones page works through an example.

What does a laboratory certificate tell me?

It names the stone, says whether it is natural or laboratory-grown, lists the treatments the laboratory could detect, and gives the weight and size. It carries no price, it cannot show that an undetectable treatment is absent, and it says nothing about your chart. A report written by the seller proves only what the seller says. The Gemstones page covers what a report shows and leaves out.
