Read the background: the introduction

Vastu, or Vastu Shastra, is the traditional Indian body of teaching on how to site and arrange a dwelling. It reads a building and its plot together, through eight directions, a centre and five elements: earth, water, fire, air and space. Many families follow it for cultural and devotional reasons, and many ask about it when they build, buy or move.

Narayani brings it to a home, a shop, an office or a plot as a conversation about the people who use the space. Who sleeps where, who cooks, where the work gets done and what already feels awkward in the day all come before any compass reading.

She is open about the limits from the start. Vastu is a tradition that many families keep, and the evidence section below sets out how far the research goes. What to change, if anything, is your decision.

What a Vastu reading looks at

  • The layout floor by floor: where rooms, doors and stairs sit, and where north falls
  • Who uses the space, at what hour, and the routine that runs through the day
  • Where the sun falls, which windows open, and where the air sits still
  • Where things belong: what the building decides, and what can be rearranged without touching a wall
  • The customs your family already keeps, so nothing is asked that does not fit them

What Vastu is and where it comes from

Vastu means a dwelling together with its plot. Vastu Shastra is the body of Sanskrit building texts that grew up around it, covering how to judge ground, place doors and rooms, and begin work at a fitting hour. Among the earliest datable texts with chapters on houses and town planning are the Arthashastra, placed between the 2nd century BCE and the 3rd century CE, and the Brihat Samhita of the 6th century CE. Later works such as the Mayamata, the Manasara and the Samarangana Sutradhara run to dozens of chapters each, and scholars date them anywhere from about the 5th to the 13th century.

Where it began is argued. Some trace it to the ritual architecture of the Vedas, while one scholar holds that the Atharvaveda verses often cited as its source give cosmic imagery and describe no developed building practice. There are regional schools too, a northern one, a southern one and Kerala’s own house-building tradition, and the sources do not always agree. So “what Vastu says” is rarely one thing, and she asks which source, which school and which household.

Why she starts with the people in the space

She begins with the household: how the day runs, who is most affected by the way the rooms are arranged, and what has already been tried. A list of faults comes later, if at all, and direction comes in as one way of looking at what you tell her.

The mind of the person living in the space comes first for her. What worries you, what you have been told about the house and what you would like to be different shape the conversation before any plan is read. Nothing in a reading is framed as a warning about what a skipped rule might bring. She asks whether any change is needed at all, then what can be done without demolition, and where a change is worth making she says why.

The ideas Vastu is built from

Six ideas carry most of what Vastu says.

IdeaWhat it means in Vastu
DirectionEight compass directions, each linked in tradition with a guardian and with certain uses.
The five elementsEarth, water, fire, air and space, which modern guides map onto parts of a plot and a house.
The centreBrahmasthan, the middle of a building, which guides keep open and uncluttered.
Layout and useWhich room sits where, and how the people in it live from morning to night.
The siteThe shape, slope, road and soil of a plot, and what stands around it.
TimingThe hour for starting work on a site or entering a new home, set with a priest or from a chart.

The mandala is a ritual grid discussed in Vastu writing. Its historical role in planning is debated rather than settled. Directions and elements is the place to explore its layout and the different scholarly views; an ordinary home need not be treated as a literal copy of that diagram.

What the evidence does and does not show

Vastu is a documented tradition of texts and practice. A search of the published research turned up no independent, controlled study showing that its directions change health, money, sleep or relationships, and you should hear that at the start.

The claimWhere it stands
Vastu as a body of old building texts and temple practiceDocumented, though dates and origins are debated.
Any direction affecting health, money, sleep or relationshipsNo independent, controlled study showing this was found. A few small studies of Vastu rules exist, and they are limited.
Daylight, fresh air and comfort shape how people feel in a spaceSupported by small studies of offices and homes. This is separate from direction, and you can work on it directly.
The mandala as a tool for laying out buildingsContested among scholars.
Ground-worship and a chosen hour for starting work (Bhoomi Pujan, muhurta)A long-standing custom. No test of what it does was found, and it sits apart from approvals and safety.
Mirrors, salt, pyramids, yantras and similar objects as correctionsThese rest on belief, with no test behind them. No health, sleep or money result should be attached to them.

Those small studies test comfort or creativity, among them a computer simulation of door directions in a warm climate and a comfort study of five Vastu-designed homes in Dubai. None is both independent and controlled, and comfort results depend on climate.

Ask something else
Can a Vastu session be held online, for a home abroad?

Yes. A plan with north marked and photographs of each room and the entrance carry most of what a Vastu reading needs, so a home in another country can be read online. A visit in person is by appointment, and the team confirms whether it is possible for your address.

What is Vastu?

Vastu is a tradition about where things belong in a building, and why: the door, the kitchen, the prayer corner, the beds and the water. Its answers come from old Sanskrit texts and from the guides written after them, and families follow it for cultural and devotional reasons as well as practical ones. Narayani offers it as a tradition and states its limits.

Is Vastu scientific?

Not in the laboratory sense. A search of the published research found no independent, controlled study showing that any direction affects health, money, sleep or relationships, and the evidence section above lists the few small studies that exist. What research does support is plainer: good daylight, fresh air and comfort make a space easier to live in. Narayani works with the tradition and with those practical points, and keeps the two apart.

Is Vastu the same for everyone, or does it depend on the people living there?

The texts speak in general terms, but a household is particular. Two families in identical flats may use the same room differently: one cooks for ten, the other orders in. Narayani reads the space against the people in it, so the same rule can carry different weight in different homes. Where guides disagree with each other, she says so and explains what she would weigh.

More questions (6)
How is Vastu different from feng shui?

Both link directions and elements to buildings, but they come from different traditions. Vastu is Indian and works with eight directions, a centre and five elements: earth, water, fire, air and space. Feng shui is Chinese. It works with *qi*, a word for breath or vital force, a map called the bagua, several compass schools and a different set of five elements: wood, fire, earth, metal and water. No independent, controlled research is known to back the directional claims of either.

Do I have to demolish or rebuild anything?

A reading does not oblige you to demolish anything. Furniture, storage and lighting can be considered without changing the structure. Practitioners disagree about whether such adjustments satisfy their traditional rules, but disagreement is no reason to frighten you into building work. Engineering requirements, permissions and affordability decide what is feasible. The corrections without demolition page separates these choices.

Does Vastu apply in a flat or a rented home?

A flat can be discussed without pretending its fixed features are yours to change. Its location in the block, entrance and pipework constrain the options. Within those limits you can consider movable furniture and daily use. Tenants also need to respect the lease and obtain any necessary permission. The home Vastu guide distinguishes rearrangements from alterations to the building.

Can Vastu be read together with my chart?

Some practitioners pair each direction with a planet and compare the result with the owner’s birth chart, a method often called Astro-Vastu. The pairings differ from one scheme to another, and no test of them is known. If you ask for it, Narayani can read a space beside your chart or your numbers, as one more way to think about your question and never as a forecast.

Who gives the guidance?

Dr. Narayani Garg looks at homes and workplaces, and where they help the question she brings Vastu together with numerology, astrology and Brain Training, her mind-first work. She asks about the people before the plan and explains her reasoning, so you can decide what to keep. The team takes the enquiries and confirms the practical details.

Do I have to believe in Vastu before I ask?

No. Some people follow it closely, some are curious, and some are asking because a relative insisted. Narayani explains what the tradition says, where its sources differ and what the evidence is, and you decide what to keep. If you would rather not follow a rule, that is a legitimate answer, and she will not press you.
