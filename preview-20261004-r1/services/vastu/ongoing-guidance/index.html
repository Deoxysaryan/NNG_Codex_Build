Ongoing Vastu guidance

A plan can be sensible at one stage and need another look after a kitchen moves, a partition is added or a branch opens. The pages below explore that kind of changing question.

This is educational background on reviewing a space over time. It does not confirm a Vastu retainer, periodic visits, an agreed message channel or access between appointments.

Narayani offers Vastu alongside mindset guidance. Ask the team whether your question fits a single consultation or any available ongoing option, and confirm the scope, format and fee before booking.

Questions to keep beside a changing plan

  • What has moved since the last stage, and what the earlier reading assumed
  • What can still be adjusted without breaking anything
  • Where a decision is waiting on a layout question
  • What is about to open, launch or move
  • How the space is really used, against how it was drawn

Why one reading can go out of date

A first reading shows a space on the day it is read, while a project keeps moving. A partition goes up, a kitchen moves, a branch opens, and each change alters what the earlier reading took for granted. Many of the small decisions in a renovation or a build are made between visits, with nobody to ask.

Explore why over time

Suppose a family renovates its flat in three stages over a year: the kitchen first, then the bedrooms, then the balcony. By the time the bedrooms are drawn, the kitchen is built, and a bedroom wall now stands where a prayer corner was planned. A reading made once, in advance, could not have known that. A conversation that runs alongside the work sees the bedroom plan when it is drawn.

A suggestion also needs time to settle. A change may be kept for a week and dropped in a busy month, and over the months the conversation shows which changes have held and which need another look. The decision stays with you.

Four situations where guidance over months fits

Explore who it suits
A growing business
A second branch, a floor added, new interiors, a new signboard. Each is a decision about a door, a seating plan or a counter, and each arrives on its own schedule. Ongoing guidance lets the first reading follow the business instead of starting again each time.
A project in phases
Design, approval, structure, fit-out, handover. The plan is not one document but a sequence, and each stage can change what the last one assumed.
A family renovating or moving
Questions about the kitchen, the bedrooms, a move date and the furniture arrive across months. A conversation alongside the work keeps each answer in step with the last.
A developer with launches
A first tower, then a second, then an amenity block. Each launch changes how the entrance, the lobby and the open space sit against the whole. The reading is revisited at each launch, with the earlier comments on the table.

Review topics you can ask about

Explore what it covers
Plan review at stages
She looks at the plans as they are redrawn: the first layout, the revised layout, the interior plan, the signage plan. Each stage is read before the next one is committed. For a shop fitting out in stages, the counter and the entrance are read when the fit-out plan arrives, and again when the lighting plan does.
Checks before decisions
A layout change, a new branch, a choice of interiors, a signboard. You can ask before the order is placed, in the form “if we do this, what changes?” A signboard is a good case: where it hangs, what it faces and what it hides can all be asked before it is made.
A seasonal or yearly review
A planned look back at set points, each season or once a year: what has been added, what is no longer used as drawn, what the next stage will touch.
Answers as questions arrive
Questions come between reviews. Some are quick, such as which wall a cabinet can use. Some need the plan in front of both of you. They are taken within a rhythm agreed at the start, so you know how to ask.
Timing windows for key steps
A muhurta is a time that a priest or a Jyotish practitioner sets as favourable for beginning something. A date they give can be weighed alongside the plan.

How to make an arrangement easy to keep up

Nothing here is required. These small habits make a longer conversation easier to keep up.

  • Keep one master plan, and mark each change on it with the date.
  • Collect questions as they arise and ask them together where you can, so that related decisions are read side by side.
  • Say what you have decided as well as what you are asking. A decision already made is part of the plan the next suggestion has to fit.
  • Tell her when you have not kept a suggestion, and why. That helps the next suggestion fit the way the space is really used.

What it does not do

Ongoing guidance is Vastu advice. It is not structural, engineering or legal advice, and it stands in for none of them. Your builder and your architect keep their responsibility for the design, the safety and the approvals, and her suggestions reach them as options they can take, change or leave. A structural question goes to an engineer, and a legal one to a lawyer.

Explore limits

If a suggestion would break a rule or a lease, the rule or the lease stands.

She makes no promise about outcomes, and nothing in an arrangement promises sales, a sale price or health. The Vastu overview page sets out where the evidence for Vastu itself stands. Light, air and comfort are the parts of a reading that can be checked on the day.

To begin ongoing guidance: what to gather

  • Plans as they stand, and each revision as it is drawn
  • Photographs of the space or site, and later of each stage
  • The address, and which way the entrance faces
  • A list of the decisions coming up, with the dates that matter
  • Contracts, tenders and accounts can wait until after the first conversation

Is this for you?

It may suit you if

  • your plans will keep moving over the next few months
  • you would rather check a decision before it is made than after
  • you want one conversation to run across the whole project

It may not be right if

  • your question is about one room on one day, where a single consultation will do
  • you want a promise about results, because none is given
  • you hope she will instruct your builder or architect, when she advises and they decide
  • your question is structural, engineering or legal, where the right professional comes first
